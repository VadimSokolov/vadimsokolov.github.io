Interpretation

The benchmark model says a horse's Center of Distribution predicts its optimal distance: more speed in the pedigree, shorter races. Horses with English Channel in the pedigree do not sit on that line. At any given CD they want about 2.53 furlongs more ground than horses without him, the vertical gap between the blue and gold lines. His contribution of stamina is not captured by the chefs de race already in his offspring's pedigrees.

The residual plot makes the same point as a misspecification check. Residuals from the benchmark fit should scatter around zero with no structure. Instead the two groups separate: horses with English Channel in the pedigree sit above zero, the rest below, and their mean residuals are about 2.5 furlongs apart. The benchmark line splits the difference between two populations. Residuals that sort by sire point to an omitted variable, and adding the English Channel indicator removes the pattern (t = 6.87, ΔR² = 0.254).

That is the statistical case for naming him a chef de race. The direction of the shift is toward stamina, which supports a Classic/Solid designation rather than Brilliant or Intermediate. Once he enters the dosage, his offspring's CD falls and their points move left along the line, absorbing part of the residual shift. His racing profile fits: a turf champion whose best wins came at 10 to 12 furlongs, and a sire who led the North American turf list in his final two seasons.

Predicted optimal distance. The augmented fit gives Ŷ = 9.00 − 2.38 CD + 2.53 d. For a horse with CD = 0.78, the benchmark predicts 9.20 − 2.80 × 0.78 = 7.0 furlongs. The augmented model predicts 7.1 furlongs without English Channel in the pedigree and 9.7 furlongs with him. In general a horse sired by English Channel (d = 1) is predicted at 11.53 − 2.38 CD furlongs, so reaching the 10 to 12 furlong races where his progeny win needs a CD between about −0.2 and 0.6.

Caveats. His runners are mostly turf horses placed in route races, so part of the shift may reflect surface and trainer choices rather than inheritance; a surface control would help. Optimal distance is inferred from races actually run, mixing aptitude with opportunity. With 80 horses the shift is estimated with a standard error of 0.37 furlongs, so the plausible range runs from roughly 1.8 to 3.3 furlongs.

    What the statistics mean, and where they break

    Each card checks a property on the pedigree you have built. Use the buttons to load a pedigree that shows the property clearly.

    CD is a weighted mean

    Give each category its score λ: +2 Brilliant, +1 Intermediate, 0 Classic, −1 Solid, −2 Professional. CD is the average score, weighted by each category's share of the points.

    CategoryPointsShareλShare × λ

    CD always lies between −2 and +2

    A weighted mean cannot leave the range of the scores. CD = +2 only when every point is Brilliant and CD = −2 only when every point is Professional. Because the scores are symmetric around 0, a balanced pedigree sits at CD = 0.

    DI is a ratio, so it can blow up

    Classic points count half to speed and half to stamina. With no Classic, Solid or Professional points the stamina side is zero and DI = ∞. With no Brilliant, Intermediate or Classic points DI = 0. With no chefs at all, neither statistic exists.

    Same DI, different CD

    DI only compares the two halves. A large Classic block and a mix of Brilliant and Solid can give the same DI = 2.00, yet the CDs differ. The paper's advice: read DI and CD together.

    Each generation carries the same weight

    Weights halve as the number of ancestors doubles: 2 × 16 = 4 × 8 = 8 × 4 = 16 × 2 = 32. Half the slots are dams, so a generation can supply at most 16 points and the whole pedigree at most 64.

    Only the generation matters, and repeats add up

    Moving a chef to another slot in the same generation leaves every statistic unchanged. A chef who appears twice through different lines (inbreeding) is counted at each appearance.

    Benchmark regression · incremental fit

    Does a candidate sire explain distance the current list misses?

    For each horse in a sample of Grade I turf winners we record Y, its optimal winning distance in furlongs (1 furlong = 1/8 mile), its CD from the current list, and d, the candidate's generation-weighted presence: d = Σ 25−g/16 over his appearances, so d = 1 if he is the sire, 0.5 a grandsire, 0.25 and 0.125 further back. The benchmark model uses CD alone. The augmented model adds d. If the list already captures everything, d should add nothing.

    From a coefficient to a category

    A chef-de-race is elevated when the t-test on d rejects and the fit improves. His category comes from converting furlongs back into CD units: λ̂ = −β̂d / |β̂CD|. The minus sign is there because more furlongs means more stamina, and stamina has negative scores.

      Dosage analysis in four steps

      A weighted tally, a weighted mean, and one extra regressor

      The dosage system turns a pedigree into a point vector, then into a ratio (DI) and a weighted mean (CD). The paper's contribution is a test for when the list itself is out of date: regress distance on CD, add the candidate, and ask whether the fit improves.

      1. Tally the chefs, weighted by generation

        Each chef-de-race in generation g adds wg = 25−g points to his category: 16, 8, 4, 2. The weights track the expected share of genes, halving each generation. English Channel's eight chefs give DP = [12, 6, 9, 5, 0], 32 points in all.

      2. Summarise with a ratio and a mean

        DI = speed points ÷ stamina points = 22.5 ÷ 9.5 = 2.37. CD = (2b + i − s − 2p) ÷ TP = 25 ÷ 32 = 0.78, the weighted mean of the scores. Roman found that Kentucky Derby winners nearly always had DI ≤ 4.00 and CD ≤ 1.00.

      3. Test the list against the data

        If the list is complete, CD captures everything the pedigree says about distance. Adding a candidate's presence d to the regression should then add nothing. For English Channel it adds β̂ = +2.53 furlongs (SE 0.37, t = 6.87), and R² rises from 0.33 to 0.59.

      4. Turn furlongs into a category

        One CD unit is worth |β̂CD| = 2.38 furlongs, so +2.53 furlongs is about −1.06 on the score scale. That is the Solid score of −1. The paper proposes a Classic/Solid designation, provisional until his sons show the same stamp.

      Dosage Profile (each chef split equally across his categories)
      DP = Σg=1..4 Σchefs j in gen g 25−g ea(j) = [b, i, c, s, p]
      Dosage Index and Center of Distribution (TP = b + i + c + s + p)
      DI = (b + i + c/2) / (c/2 + s + p)
      DI = ∞ if c/2 + s + p = 0
      CD = (2b + i − s − 2p) / TP ∈ [−2, +2]

      Y = α + βCD CD + ε
      Y = α + βCD CD + βd d + ε, ε ~ N(0, σ²)
      Tests, with n − 3 residual degrees of freedom
      t = β̂d / SE(β̂d)
      F = ΔR² / [(1 − R²aug)/(n − 3)] = t²
      Category assignment
      λ̂ = −β̂d / |β̂CD|
      SE(λ̂) = SE(β̂d) / |β̂CD|
      category = the score λk nearest to λ̂
      dual if λ̂ is within 1 SE of a boundary

      Takeaway for students. CD is a weighted mean and the benchmark regression is a test of omitted information: if a variable the current summary ignores still predicts the outcome, the summary is incomplete. The same logic tests whether any index, score or rating has missed something.

      Where dosage comes from

      Around 1910 the French cavalry officer J.J. Vuillier tallied the stallions that kept appearing in champions' pedigrees. In 1974 Franco Varola sorted these chefs-de-race into five ordered types, from Brilliant (pure speed) to Professional (pure stamina). In 1981 Steve Roman turned the idea into the point system, DI and CD used here. Steve Miller has kept the list since about 2016; it holds about 230 sires.

      The sires and categories on this page are those printed in the paper's English Channel example.

      Limitations the paper states

      • The list is backward-looking. A sire is added only after several generations of evidence, so the system mismeasures horses carrying his blood in the meantime.
      • The four-generation window is a practical choice; older records are less reliable.
      • Roman's DI ≤ 4.00 and CD ≤ 1.00 limits are empirical. They shift probabilities and do not forbid a win.
      • The sample should come from one competition stratum, Y should be the mode or median of winning distances, and injured horses should be left out.
      • The sign of β̂ should hold across subsamples such as colts and fillies, or dirt and turf.
      • English Channel's sons are only starting at stud, so the multigenerational test is still open and the designation is provisional.

      What is real and what is simulated

      The pedigree example, the category of each chef, the point allocation and the DI and CD values come from the paper's Table 4 and equations (11) and (12).

      The regression data are simulated, as they are in the paper. The default sample is the exact draw from the paper's own code, with 40 of 80 horses carrying English Channel. It reproduces Table 5's coefficient on d, standard error, t, benchmark R², ΔR² and F, but not its intercepts or benchmark CD slope. A second sample is tuned to match every printed digit. The points in the summary plots at the top are fresh draws. The hypothetical sires in the scenario list are teaching cases, not claims about real stallions.

      The facts table at the top comes with the analysis and was not checked against race records here.

      The paper suggests Kitten's Joy, Medaglia d'Oro, Tapit and War Front as the next sires to test.

      Try this in class

      1. Show English Channel's pedigree with the chefs cleared, and give students the paper's list of eight chefs and their categories. Ask them to compute the Dosage Profile by hand, then load the example to check: [12, 6, 9, 5, 0], DI = 2.37, CD = 0.78.
      2. Load "Balanced: big Classic" and then "Polarised: Brilliant + Solid". Both have DI = 2.00. Ask which pedigree is more speed-biased and whether each passes the Derby limits. The polarised one sits exactly on CD = 1.00.
      3. Move Northern Dancer to another great-great-grandparent slot, then to a great-grandparent slot. Ask students to predict each change before you make it. The first changes nothing; the second doubles his 2 points to 4 and moves CD.
      4. Set the scenario to "no effect" and run 1,000 samples. About 5% reject, the test's size. Switch to the weak +0.6 sire: at n = 80, with half the horses carrying him, power is near 0.32. Ask the class how many horses are needed for 80% power, then find it with the slider (about 270).
      5. Load "A foal by English Channel" and tick the box that counts him as Classic/Solid. CD drops from 0.83 to 0.07. Compare with the regression: +2.53 furlongs ÷ 2.38 furlongs per CD unit is a shift of about 1.06 CD units for a horse he sired. Which is closer to the stamina his offspring show?
