Reading dummies and interactions

Every coefficient is a difference from the baseline

The baseline is Dominick's in a week without a feature ad. R picks the first level of a factor as the reference, and Dominick's comes first alphabetically. The intercept and the log(price) slope describe the baseline line. Every other coefficient says how a brand or the ad shifts that line.

  1. A dummy shifts the intercept

    A dummy is 1 for its category and 0 otherwise. With brand dummies and one price slope, the three lines are parallel.

  2. A dummy × price term shifts the slope

    Multiply a dummy by log(price) and that category gets its own slope. The coefficient on log(price):brandtropicana is Tropicana's elasticity minus Dominick's.

  3. An interaction makes one effect depend on another

    With feat × log(price), the price elasticity is β₁ without an ad and β₁ + β₃ with one. In the reference fit that is −0.96 and −0.96 − 0.98 = −1.94: featuring doubles price sensitivity.

  4. Leaving out a variable moves the others

    Model 1 and the price × feature model have no brand dummies, so the price slope also picks up brand differences: Tropicana costs more and sells more. Its elasticity looks small, about −1.5 in model 1 and −0.96 in the reference fit. Add the brand dummies and the within-brand elasticity is near −3.

  5. Significant is not the same as important

    With thousands of store-weeks, small differences get large t-statistics. Ask whether the size of the effect would change a pricing decision. A difference of 0.05 in elasticity rarely does.

Takeaway for students. Read each coefficient as "compared with the baseline, holding the other predictors fixed". Then turn the model into lines: write out the equation for each brand and ad status, and report the elasticities in plain English. A caveat: features often coincide with price cuts, so the ad effect is partly a price effect.

The MLR model

βⱼ is the effect of a one-unit increase in Xⱼ holding all other predictors fixed, the marginal effect.

Y = β₀ + β₁X₁ + … + βₚXₚ + ε

Newfood

A dummy in a log model is a percent lift. High advertising (adv = 1) multiplies sales by e^0.15 ≈ 1.16, a 16% lift, but its t = 1.49 is below 2. The price elasticity is −1.74.

The simulated truth

Prices center near $1.65, $2.23 and $3.00; about 30%, 25% and 15% of weeks are featured, at prices 10% lower. Noise SD 0.70.

Try this in class

  1. Start with model 1. Ask what an elasticity of about −1.5 means for the store. Then switch to model 2 and watch the slope fall to about −3. Ask why adding brand changed the price effect.
  2. In model 2, read brandtropicana. Turn it into a percent with e^b and say it in a sentence: at the same price Tropicana sells about 4.6 times as much as Dominick's.
  3. Switch to model 3. Are the brand slopes really different? Look at the t-statistics, then raise n to 6,000 and look again.
  4. Switch to model 4 and read the line cards. Which brand is most price sensitive when featured? Move the "true feat × price effect" slider to 0 and see whether the dashed lines become parallel to the solid ones.
  5. Compare the price × feature model with model 4. Both have a feat × price term. Why is the no-ad elasticity about −0.96 in one and about −2.3 for Dominick's in the other?
