Hypothesis testing building blocks

Power is decided before the data arrive

The test rejects when z passes the critical value. α is the red area beyond that line under H0. Power is the green area beyond the same line under H1. Everything that pushes the two curves apart, a bigger effect, a bigger sample or a smaller spread, raises power.

  1. Set up H0 and H1

    H0 is "no effect". The red curve is the distribution of z if H0 is true: N(0, 1).

  2. Choose α

    α is the false-positive rate you accept: 0.05 in business, 0.01 in clinical trials, 5σ in physics. It fixes the critical value:

  3. The test statistic

    z = (estimate − null value) / SE. If the true effect is δ, z is centred at λ = δ / SE instead of 0.

  4. Decision rule

    Two-sided: reject if |z| > zα/2. One-sided: reject if z > zα. Choose the side before looking at the data.

  5. Power

    Power is the chance of rejecting H0 when the effect is real.

  6. p-value and CI

    These come after the data (step 7). Power tells you whether a non-significant result means "no effect" or "too small a test".

Takeaway for students. Most A/B tests in industry fail because the sample is too small to detect anything. Pepsi's 56 out of 100 had only about a one-in-three chance of reaching significance even if the 6-point lead were real. Required n grows with the square of 1/MDE: halving the effect you want to detect quadruples the sample.

Karaca: how big a sample?

Baseline 3%, MDE 0.5 pp (a 17% relative lift), α = 0.05 two-sided, 80% power. About 19,700 sessions per arm, 40,000 in total: a few days of Karaca's online traffic.

p̄ = 0.03 + 0.005/2 = 0.0325

Coke vs Pepsi: why 100 is not enough

z = 1.2 fell short of 1.645. To detect a 6-point lead with 80% power you need about 430 tasters. Pepsi's real campaign used thousands per market.

SE₀ = √(0.5 · 0.5 / 100) = 0.05

Where the quantiles come from

zα/2 puts α/2 in each tail of N(0, 1). zβ is the point with β below it. Textbooks often round z0.80 = 0.8416 to 0.84; the lab uses exact quantiles, so its n can differ by a few units.

1.96 = z0.025, 1.645 = z0.05, 0.84 = z0.20

Try this in class

  1. Load Coke vs Pepsi. Power is about 33%. Ask: if Pepsi really is preferred by 56%, how often does a 100-person test show it? Then press "Set n to the required size" and watch the green curve move past the critical line.
  2. Load Karaca and halve the MDE to 0.25 pp. The required n roughly quadruples to about 78,000 per arm. Double it to 1 pp and n drops to about 5,000.
  3. Keep Karaca and move α from 0.05 to 0.01. The red area shrinks, the critical line moves right, and power falls. Ask what it costs in sessions to get the 80% back.
  4. Switch Karaca from two-sided to one-sided. The required n falls by about a fifth. Discuss why two-sided is still the usual recommendation.
  5. Raise the baseline from 3% to 30% with the MDE at 0.5 pp. p̄(1 − p̄) grows, so the same absolute lift needs many more users. Then try the same 17% relative lift (5 pp).
