The bootstrap

Resample the rows, refit, repeat

The bootstrap never uses the SE formula. It treats the sample as the population, draws n rows with replacement, and refits. The spread of those refits estimates the standard error.

Two intervals, two questions

The confidence interval asks where the average response at Xf is. The prediction interval asks where the next single observation will fall. Same point estimate, different width, because a new observation brings its own error ε.

  1. The PI is wider by the noise σ²

    The variance of a prediction error splits into two parts. σ² is the noise in the new observation, and no amount of data removes it. σ²(1/n + (Xf − x̄)²/Sxx) is the error from estimating the line, and it shrinks as n grows. The CI only has the second part.

  2. Prediction is worst far from x̄

    Both bands are narrowest at x̄ and flare out as Xf moves away. Extrapolating beyond the data makes the estimation part grow quickly, especially when the x values are bunched together and Sxx is small.

  3. β̂₁ is a random variable

    Redraw the data and β̂₁ changes. Its histogram centers on the true β₁ (unbiasedness), has the predicted spread σ/√Sxx, and is normal. These are facts you can check, not assumptions.

  4. 95% means about 19 in 20

    Each replication builds its own interval. About 1 in 20 misses the true β₁. The misses are drawn in red, and the coverage rate settles near 0.95 as M grows.

  5. The bootstrap checks the formula on real data

    For mtcars the classical SE is 0.559 and the bootstrap SE is about 0.71. The gap is a sign that constant variance is strained: heavier cars vary more. The bootstrap is roughly robust to that, because it resamples whole rows.

Takeaway for students. For prediction, a low s matters more than a high R². The PI can never be narrower than about ±2σ, however much data you collect. Hand someone a DGP with the numbers blanked out and ask them to make the estimator find the truth: statistics becomes something they do.

Try this in class

  1. Ask students to guess how wide the PI is at x̄ compared with the CI. Then read the table: with n = 50 the PI is about 7 times wider. Raise n to 200 and the CI shrinks while the PI barely moves.
  2. Drag Xf from 0 out to 5. Watch the estimation share of the bar grow. Then set w = 0.5 and repeat: with bunched x values, extrapolation gets expensive fast.
  3. Press "Replicate 1,000 times" and compare the Monte Carlo sd with σ/√Sxx. Double n and predict the new sd before you press again (it drops by about √2).
  4. Count the red intervals in the coverage plot. Ask whether 3 or 8 misses out of 100 would be surprising, then add 10,000 replications and read the coverage.
  5. Switch the bootstrap to mtcars. The bootstrap SE comes out near 0.71, above the classical 0.559. Ask what feature of the scatter could cause that.
