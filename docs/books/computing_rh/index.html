Computing the Riemann Hypothesis

By Nicholas G. Polson and Vadim Sokolov.

Volumes

  • Computing the Riemann Hypothesis, Volume I: Representations of Xi, Thorin Measures and the Reciprocal Xi Function. PDF. DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23861971.
  • Computing the Riemann Hypothesis, Volume II: Cauchy Deconvolution and Certified Computation. PDF. DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23861976.

Description

The Riemann Hypothesis can be restated as the positivity of a measure, the Thorin measure of a ratio of values of Riemann’s Xi function. This book computes that measure from Pólya’s integral for Xi, without primes or a list of zeros, in certified ball arithmetic, and asks what such computations can and cannot teach about the zeros.

A certified zero off the critical line would refute the hypothesis, but no finite computation can prove it. From the zeros verified up to height 3 × 10^12 the book certifies that the derivatives of the heat trace have the predicted signs up to order 1.16 × 10^25, and proves that such finite positivity tests cannot settle it: a perturbation of Xi with the same zeros below a large height, and zeros off the line above it, passes them. No proof of the hypothesis is claimed.

New results: the reciprocal Xi function at x to the power p is hyperbolically completely monotone exactly when the absolute value of p is at most one minus twice the largest zero angle over pi; at the basepoint one the Thorin density is certified smallest at the origin, so the shifted clock is a stable variable plus a generalized gamma convolution; between the basepoints one half and one the shifted clocks are almost generalized gamma convolutions, unconditionally; the clock of Pólya’s Bessel function is the hitting time of an explicit diffusion; adjacent real zeros of a polynomial cannot meet under the backward heat flow before an explicit time; and the Pólya law lies within 2 × 10^-6 in total variation of the ferromagnetic laws of Lee and Yang, a distance that is zero exactly under the hypothesis.

The book is in two volumes. Volume I, Representations of Xi, Thorin Measures and the Reciprocal Xi Function, develops the theory: the analytic and probabilistic representations of Xi, infinite divisibility, generalised gamma convolutions and the Bondesson hierarchy, the heat trace, the reciprocal zeta function, the basepoints and the critical strip, Dedekind eta and Dirichlet L functions, hitting times and Krein strings, extreme value and ferromagnetic laws with a two spin Lee and Yang form of the hypothesis, Pólya and Haseo Ki on the zeros of entire functions, and a route to the Riemann Hypothesis assembled from the whole book. Volume II, Cauchy Deconvolution and Certified Computation, holds the computations: what computation can and cannot decide, Xi in ball arithmetic, the Thorin measure at finite resolution, the de Bruijn Newman flow and the barrier method in Thorin form, the Thorin phase diagram, Cauchy deconvolution from the level one measure down to the zeros (the backward Cauchy flow, the explicit formula as a deconvolution, particle filters and the Balazard, Saias and Yor criterion by Monte Carlo), Weil positivity in the windows of Yoshida and Zhu, truncations and friable approximants of Xi with certified real zeros, approximating clocks, and conditional results under a quasi Riemann hypothesis.

The October 2026 claims of a zero free half plane Re s > 7/8 for all L functions, and the later preprints that lower the constant slightly, are not refereed: the book states only conditional consequences (a principal signal for zeta, the sliver and Mertens estimate, the Thorin property at every basepoint above 7/8), credited to their authors.

The scripts and data files behind the tables and figures are in the book’s repository https://github.com/VadimSokolov/computing_rh, listed in code/README.md.

Main results

  • Bosch type theorem for the reciprocal Xi function (Volume I, Theorem 9.3.5, page 139): The reciprocal Xi function composed with the power x to the p is HCM exactly when the absolute value of p is at most one minus twice the largest zero angle over pi, so RH is the extreme case p equal to one.
  • Derivative signs of the heat trace up to order 1.16 x 10^25 (Volume I, Theorem 8.1.13, page 114): From the verification of RH to height 3 times 10^12, every derivative of the heat trace alternates in sign, equivalently the Williamson measures are positive, up to order 1.16 times 10^25, with the numerical inequalities of the proof certified in ball arithmetic.
  • Biane form lower bound for the collision time of zeros under the backward heat flow (Volume II, Theorem 4.1.9, page 61): While all zeros of a real polynomial stay real and simple, a gap between two adjacent zeros cannot close under the backward heat flow before the time one over the minimum of Laguerre’s quantity over the gap, a bound at least as strong as the one from the critical point and attained by z squared minus a squared.
  • Clock of Pólya’s Bessel function as a hitting time of an explicit diffusion (Volume I, Theorem 18.1.10, page 513): The index side clock of Pólya’s Bessel function, the GGC with Thorin atoms at the squares of the positive zeros of nu maps to K_{i nu}(2 pi), is the clock of a hitting time of a level by an explicit diffusion, planar Brownian motion killed at rate one half and Doob transformed by K_0.
  • Minimum of the Thorin density at the origin and stable summand at the basepoint one (Volume I, Proposition 12.1.11, page 221): With no hypothesis on the zeros, certified with the first 1800 zeros in ball arithmetic and explicit zero counts above height 2000, the Thorin density of the shifted clock at the basepoint one is smallest only at the origin, so the clock is the sum of an independent positive stable one half variable and a generalized gamma convolution.
  • Almost GGC theorem for the shifted clocks (Volume I, Theorem 14.3.2, page 339): With no hypothesis, for every basepoint between one half and one the Cauchy kernels of the zeros to the right of the basepoint carry a vanishing fraction of the mass of the kernels of the other zeros, with a power saving from zero density estimates.
  • Total variation distance from the Pólya law to ferromagnetic laws is below two millionths, with no hypothesis (Volume II, Proposition 19.1.15, page 532): With no hypothesis the total variation distance from the Pólya law to the ferromagnetic laws is below 1.932 × 10^-6 by a certified computation in ball arithmetic and below 1.266 × 10^-4 by an explicit construction, below 6.024 × 10^-6 by a fitted law on a Bessel kernel (a note of Nicholas Polson), and it is zero exactly when the Riemann Hypothesis holds.
  • Threshold theorem along the flow for the shifted clocks of the heat flow function (Volume II, Theorem 6.2.3, page 97): For every time \(t\ge0\) and basepoint \(\alpha\ge\frac12\), the shifted clock of the heat flow function \(\xi_t\) is the Laplace transform of a generalized gamma convolution exactly when no zero of \(\xi_t\) has real part above \(\alpha\), and its Thorin measure is then an explicit sum of Cauchy kernels and atoms over the zeros.
  • Phase diagram theorem, de Bruijn’s strip theorem in Thorin form (Volume II, Theorem 6.3.2, page 102): The set of points \((t,\alpha)\) where the shifted clock of the heat flow function is a generalized gamma convolution is carried along parabolas by the flow (de Bruijn’s strip theorem in Thorin form), the de Bruijn Newman constant is the minimum of \(t+2(\alpha-\frac12)^2\) over it, and RH holds exactly when the corner \((0,\frac12)\) belongs to it.
  • Unconditional GGC property of the reciprocal Xi function in the fourth root variable, compensated by the first power sum, with explicit Thorin density from the heat trace (Volume I, Proposition 12.1.7, page 218): Unconditionally, the function \(\Psi(s)=\frac{\xi(\frac12+s^{1/4})}{\xi(\frac12)}e^{-p_1\sqrt s}\) is the Laplace transform of a generalised gamma convolution whose Thorin measure has an explicit density built from the heat trace \(W\), and under RH it equals the Weierstrass product \(\prod_{\gamma>0}(1+\sqrt s/\gamma^2)e^{-\sqrt s/\gamma^2}\).
  • RH is equivalent to boundedness of the heat trace of the zeros on a vertical line (Volume I, Theorem 8.1.4, page 100): The Riemann Hypothesis holds if and only if the heat trace \(v\mapsto W(\sigma+iv)\) of the zeros is bounded on the real line, for one, equivalently every, \(\sigma>0\).
  • Concentration of the Laguerre expressions at the origin along the whole de Bruijn Newman flow, with positivity of \(L_{n,t}\) on every window for large \(n\) (Newton 2026, Zenodo) (Volume II, Theorem 4.1.21, page 74): For the probability measures \(\mathbb{E}_{n,t}\) defining the Laguerre expressions \(L_{n,t}\) of the de Bruijn Newman function, the second moment of the centred variable tends to zero uniformly in \(0\le t\le\frac12\), so for every \(X\) the expression \(L_{n,t}(x)\) is positive on \(|x|\le X\) for all large \(n\), uniformly in \(t\) (Newton 2026, Zenodo); the index is not effective.
  • RH holds if and only if the density of the clock is a Pólya frequency function of infinite order (Volume I, Proposition 5.2.2, page 45): RH is equivalent to the density \(f\) of the clock being a Pólya frequency function of infinite order, the one sided form of the two sided criterion: under RH \(f\) is the density of \(\sum_\gamma e_\gamma/\gamma^2\), and conversely Schoenberg’s theorem forces \(G\) to have only real zeros.
  • RH holds if and only if a freely infinitely divisible law nu_xi with free cumulant transform log xi(1/2)/xi(1/2+sqrt(-z)) exists (Volume I, Proposition 14.2.4, page 326): RH holds exactly when the function \(\mathcal{C}_\xi(z)=\log\{\xi(\frac12)/\xi(\frac12+\sqrt{-z})\}\) is the free cumulant transform of a freely infinitely divisible probability measure, which under RH is \(\Lambda_{\rm BP}(\mu_0)\), with compact support, free cumulants \(m_{n-1}/n\) and free Lévy measure \(N(y^{-1/2})dy/y\).
  • No reweighting of Riemann’s theta series by summable weights has infinitely many real zeros (Volume II, Theorem 18.2.4, page 484): For nonzero real weights \(w_n\) with \(\sum|w_n|n^{-\sigma}<\infty\) for every \(\sigma>0\) (this includes the friable truncations), the reweighted function has finitely many real zeros and infinitely many nonreal zeros.
  • Paired form: zeta has no zero to the right of alpha exactly when the real and imaginary parts of xi on the line alpha interlace (Volume II, Theorem 18.14.3, page 509): For \(\alpha>\frac12\), zeta has no zero with real part at least \(\alpha\) exactly when \(E_\alpha(z)=\xi(\alpha-iz)\) is Hermite Biehler, exactly when the real and imaginary parts of \(\xi(\alpha+it)\) have only real simple strictly interlacing zeros; hence RH holds if and only if this holds for every \(\alpha>\frac12\).
  • The density of the reciprocal xi law is the limit of grouped residue sums, with no hypothesis on the zeros (Volume I, Theorem 5.3.1, page 51): There are radii \(R_n\in[n,2n]\) with \(f(y)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{|u_\rho|<R_n}\operatorname{Res}_{u=u_\rho}e^{uy}/G(u)\) for every \(y>0\), with an explicit error bound, whatever the zeros.
  • The Euler hitting times converge after scaling to a spectrally positive stable law of index 3/2 (Volume II, Theorem 21.3.4, page 587): As \(P\to\infty\), \(Z_P=(Y_P-\kappa_2(P))/\kappa_3(P)^{2/3}\Rightarrow Z\) with \(\log\mathbb Ee^{-sZ}=(2s)^{3/2}/6\), with the bound \(|\log\mathbb Ee^{-sZ_P}-(2s)^{3/2}/6|\le\frac{|s|^2}6\rho_4(P)\) for \(\operatorname{Re}s\ge0\) and \(\rho_4(P)\to0\).
  • Thorin rigidity: any factorisation of the reciprocal xi law into two GGC laws forces RH and splits the zeros (Volume II, Theorem 21.4.1, page 588): If \(\mu=\mu_1*\mu_2\) with both factors GGC, then RH holds and the Thorin measures are \(\sum a_\gamma\delta_{\gamma^2}\) and \(\sum(m_\gamma-a_\gamma)\delta_{\gamma^2}\) with \(0\le a_\gamma\le m_\gamma\); no GGC law with drift or a nonzero absolutely continuous Thorin part is a GGC factor with a GGC cofactor.
  • Prime built GGC hitting times converge to the base point laws for theta < beta < 2 - theta under QRH(theta) (Volume I, Theorem 5.8.4, page 67): Assume QRH(\(\theta\)) and \(\theta<\beta<2-\theta\). Then \(|d_X(t)-\pi^{-1}\operatorname{Re}\xi'/\xi(\beta+it)|\ll_\beta X^{\theta-\beta}\log(t+2)\) uniformly in \(t\), \(d_X\ge0\) for \(X\ge X_0(\beta,\theta)\), the GGC laws \(\mu^{(\beta)}_X\) with Thorin density \(d_X\) converge weakly to \(\mu^{(\beta)}\), and conversely nonnegativity of \(d_X(t)\) for large \(X\) at one \(\beta\) gives QRH(\(\beta\)). Status: conditional on the quasi Riemann hypothesis QRH(\(\theta\)) for some \(\theta<1\) (every nontrivial zero has real part at most \(\theta\)).
  • Each term of the theta series kernel is an explicit combination of two Gumbel kernels of fractional order (Volume I, Theorem 19.17.1, page 556): With \(x=-4u-\log(\pi n^2)\), \(\phi_n(u)\,du=\pi^{-1/4}n^{-1/2}\Gamma(\frac54)[\frac58g_{9/4}(x)-\frac34g_{5/4}(x)]\,dx\) with \(g_k(x)=e^{-kx}\exp(-e^{-x})/\Gamma(k)\), and its Fourier transform is \(\frac14n^{-s}\xi(s)/\zeta(s)\) with \(s=(1+iz)/2\).
  • All zeros of Xi are real if and only if the reciprocal xi law is a GGC, and real and simple if and only if it is the hitting time of a string (Volume I, Corollary 19.19.1, page 562): RH holds if and only if \(L\) is the Laplace transform of a generalised gamma convolution with positive Thorin measure \(\sum_k\delta_{\gamma_k^2}\); the zeros are all real and simple if and only if \(L\) is the Laplace transform of the hitting time of a Krein string with positive mass; the same holds for any even real entire \(\widehat H\) of order below two with \(\widehat H(0)>0\).
  • Energy form of RH: the normalised prime error has finite discounted energy if and only if RH holds (Volume II, Proposition 15.9.4, page 379): RH holds if and only if \(\int_0^\infty D(u)^2e^{-2\epsilon u}du<\infty\) for every \(\epsilon>0\); under RH the mean square of \(D\) per unit of \(u\) tends to \(2\sum_{\gamma>0}m_\gamma^2/(\frac14+\gamma^2)\), which is \(2+\gamma_E-\log4\pi=0.0461914179\) for simple zeros.
  • One heat time suffices: RH holds if and only if the sine squared functional is nonnegative for every shift at a single heat time (Volume II, Theorem 15.10.1, page 382): For any fixed \(t>0\), RH holds if and only if \(V(t,a)=\sum_j2\sin^2(a\gamma_j/2)e^{-\lambda_jt}\ge0\) for every \(a\ge0\); the converse is by Landau’s theorem for Laplace transforms of nonnegative functions.
  • The tilted uniform law has a strictly positive Thorin density for every positive tilt, a Lorentzian smoothing of the lattice 2 pi m (Volume II, Theorem 20.4.4, page 564): For every \(\delta>0\), \(\pi\kappa_\delta(y)=\frac{y^2}{\delta(\delta^2+y^2)}-\frac12\coth\frac\delta2\frac{1-\cos y}{\cosh\delta-\cos y}=\sum_{m\ge1}[\ell_\delta(y-2\pi m)+\ell_\delta(y+2\pi m)-2\ell_\delta(2\pi m)]>0\) for \(y>0\), so the compensated tilted reciprocal of \(\varphi_U(s)=(1-e^{-s})/s\) is a GGC transform, and as \(\delta\downarrow0\) the Thorin measure tends to \(\sum_{m\ge1}\delta_{2\pi m}\) vaguely.
  • The shifted zeta equals (2 pi) to the s times the Mellin transform of the positive Thorin density of the tilted uniform law (Volume II, Theorem 20.4.5, page 565): For \(1<\operatorname{Re}s<3\) and every \(\delta>0\), \(\zeta_\delta(s)=(2\pi)^s\int_0^\infty y^{-s}\kappa_\delta(y)\,dy\), and at \(\delta=0\) the right side is formally \(\zeta(s)\); formally the identity of Comtet and Majumdar is the Mellin transform of the lattice Thorin measure \(\sum_m\delta_{2\pi m}\) of the uniform law, divergent for \(0<s<1\), and the shift smooths it into a positive density.
  • The sign of the real part of the drift is the sign of minus sin Theta m times the derivative of the Hardy function of chi 4 (Volume II, Theorem 17.4.1, page 460): For a simple zero \(\rho=\frac12+i\gamma\), \(\gamma>0\), of \(\xi_4\) with \(z_0=\rho/2\) and \(\delta_m\) the drift coefficient of the zeros of the truncated Mellin transforms of the beta lattice, \(\delta_m=iC_m(\gamma)e^{i\Theta_m(\gamma)}/Z_\beta'(\gamma)\) with \(C_m>0\) and \(\Theta_m=\vartheta_\beta(\gamma)+\arg L(\rho+2m+2,\chi_4)-\sum_{j=0}^m[\arctan\frac{1/2+2j}\gamma+\arctan\frac{3/2+2j}\gamma]\), so \(\operatorname{sgn}\Re\delta_m=\operatorname{sgn}[-\sin\Theta_m\,Z_\beta'(\gamma)]\).
  • The normalised Hermite coefficient equals Xi at the turning point up to an explicit error of order a to the fourth (Volume II, Theorem 17.9.1, page 470): For \(0<a\le a_0\) and \(m\ge0\), \(|b_m(a)/A_m(a)-\Xi(t_m)|\le M_4(a_0)a^4/12\) and \(b_m/A_m=\Xi(t_m)+a^4Q(t_m)+R_m(a)\) with \(|R_m|\le M_8(a_0)a^8/672\), where \(Q(t)=\frac{\Xi'''(t)}{6t}-\frac{\Xi''(t)}{4t^2}+\frac{\Xi'(t)}{4t^3}\) and \(Q(0)=\frac1{12}\int\Phi u^4du\).
  • In one weight the secant dilates over any set of dilations with divergent reciprocal sum are complete, whatever the zeros of the beta function do (Volume I, Theorem 17.3.2, page 478): For \(c>0\) and \(\Lambda\subset[1,\infty)\) with \(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}1/\lambda=\infty\), \(\{\operatorname{sech}(\lambda x)\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\) is complete in \(\mathcal W_c\), unconditionally, in particular for the integer dilates and the prime dilates, at \(c=\frac12\) included; the proof is Carleman’s formula for a half plane and uses no arithmetic.
  • Two weight completeness of the secant dilates is equivalent to a zero free half plane for L(s,chi_4) and to convergence of the Möbius secant series (Volume I, Theorem 17.5.2, page 480): For \(\frac12\le\theta<1\) the following are equivalent: \(L(s,\chi_4)\ne0\) for \(\Re s>\theta\); \(\{\operatorname{sech}(nx)\}\) is complete in \(\mathcal W_a\cap\mathcal W_b\) for all \(\theta<a<b<1\); \(S_N\to e^{-x}\) in \(\mathcal W_c\) for every \(c\in(\theta,1)\). With \(\theta=\frac12\) the first is the generalised Riemann hypothesis for \(L(s,\chi_4)\).
  • The Thorin measure of reciprocal xi is absolutely continuous with the explicit density (1/pi) Re xi’/xi at every level from one (Volume II, Theorem 10.1.1, page 167): For every level \(\theta\ge1\) the measure \(\sum_\rho\mathrm{Cauchy}(\gamma_\rho,\theta-\sigma_\rho)\) has real analytic density \(\pi^{-1}\operatorname{Re}(\xi'/\xi)(\theta+iv)\), at \(\theta=1\) a gamma part minus a compensated prime sum, valid also at \(v=0\).
  • Dichotomy for the band limited backward Cauchy flow from level one: convergence at level theta’ if and only if zeta has no zeros to the right of theta’ (Volume II, Theorem 10.3.1, page 171): The Fejér band limited backward flow from the level one measure converges to the level \(\theta'\) Thorin measure if \(\zeta\) has no zeros in \(\operatorname{Re}s>\theta'\), and has no measure limit if it has a zero there, so the Riemann hypothesis is convergence at every \(\theta'>\frac12\).
  • What EM computes for the backward Cauchy flow: the Thorin measure at level theta’ when zeta has no zeros to the right of theta’ (Volume II, Proposition 11.3.4, page 197): Under three growth conditions on the grids, the EM limits converge vaguely to the Thorin measure at level \(\theta'\) with residual tending to zero if \(\zeta\) has no zeros in \(\operatorname{Re}s>\theta'\), and the residual stays bounded away from zero if it has one.
  • The reciprocal xi transform at level theta is a GGC if and only if zeta has no zeros right of theta, if and only if the real part of xi’/xi is positive there (Volume II, Theorem 11.9.1, page 209): For \(\frac12\le\theta<1\): \(F_\theta(s)=\xi(\theta)/\xi(\theta+\sqrt s)\) is a GGC Laplace transform if and only if \(\zeta\) has no zeros in \(\operatorname{Re}s>\theta\), if and only if \(\operatorname{Re}(\xi'/\xi)>0\) on \(\operatorname{Re}s>\theta\); for \(\theta=\frac12\) the last equivalence is Lagarias’s theorem.
  • Certified positivity of u_{3/4} and u_{7/8} on [0,1420] and 1000 zeros on the critical line below 1419.9, in ball arithmetic (Volume II, Theorem 11.9.6, page 216): A computation in ball arithmetic at two precisions (96 and 160 bits) certifies that \(u_{3/4}\) and \(u_{7/8}\) are positive on \([0,1420]\) and that \(\xi\) has exactly \(1000\) zeros below height \(1419.9\), all on the critical line and simple; this is a computer assisted result covering only this range, and the count of zeros is known (Platt and Trudgian). Status: computed.
  • The real part of an off line pair of zeros is resolved only at the rate tau to the one half (Volume II, Theorem 12.5.2, page 257): Because the functional equation forces off line zeros to come in pairs at one half plus and minus delta and the data depend on delta only through delta squared, the real part is resolved at the rate (tau/norm of t_perp)^{1/2} for data accurate to tau, and no test, whatever the method, does better.
  • Fifty three certified zeros on the critical line in a window at height 1e13 plus 100, above the verified range (Volume II, Result 12.8.7, page 272): A ball arithmetic scan certifies 53 sign changes of Hardy’s function, hence 53 zeros of zeta on the critical line, in a window centred at 1e13 plus 100, above the range verified by Platt and Trudgian; the count of zeros in the window and the real parts are not certified, and nothing is certified off the line. Status: computed.
  • The critical width of a positive datum for Cauchy deconvolution is attained when finite (Volume II, Proposition 7.2.2, page 127): For a nonnegative \(u\) with \(\int u/(1+x^2)<\infty\), a positive source at width \(c\) exists for every finite \(c\le c^*(u)\) and for no larger \(c\), where \(c^*(u)\) is the supremum of the widths with a positive source, which is attained when finite.
  • Sharp Hölder exponent (b+w)/(a+w) for Cauchy deconvolution of finite signed sources (Volume II, Proposition 7.3.2, page 129): For a finite signed measure \(\nu\) of total variation at most \(B\) and \(h=P_w*\nu\), \(\|P_b*h\|_2\le\sqrt2D^{1-\kappa}\|P_a*h\|_2^\kappa\) for \(w\ge0\) and \(-w<b<a\), with \(\kappa=(b+w)/(a+w)\) and \(D=B/\sqrt{2\pi(b+w)}\), and no larger exponent holds uniformly over this class.
  • The negative part of the Thorin density at level theta is bounded by the number of zeros to the right of theta, and is a fraction T to the minus (theta minus one half) over four of its mass unconditionally (Volume II, Theorem 13.7.1, page 295): For \(\frac12<\theta<1\), \(\int_0^Tu_\theta^-\le N(\theta,T+\frac12)\) and \(\mathfrak U_\theta((0,T])=\frac T{2\pi}\log\frac T{2\pi e}-2N(\theta,T)+O(\log T)\), so with Selberg’s density theorem the negative part is a fraction \(O(T^{-(\theta-1/2)/4})\) of the mass, unconditionally.
  • No admissible pair of a cosine polynomial and a level kernel gives a zero free region wider than a constant below one over log gamma (Volume II, Proposition 13.8.7, page 305): If an admissible pair certifies \(y\) then \(y\le\frac2A(a_1-a_0-a_0\log\frac{a_1}{a_0})<\frac{2(a_1-a_0)}A\le1\), which is \(0.0548\) for \(3+4\cos\omega+\cos2\omega\) and \(0.1051\) for the degree four polynomial of the chapter; the density of the zeros through the gamma factor caps the scheme.
  • The Balazard, Saias and Yor criterion at every level theta and every height: the Cauchy average of log of zeta minus log of h equals the sum of Green potentials of the zeros right of the level (Volume II, Theorem 14.2.1, page 320): For every \(\theta\in[\frac12,1)\) the Cauchy average of \(\log|\zeta|\) on the line \(\operatorname{Re}s=\theta\), minus \(\log|h_\theta|\) at the point, is the sum of the Green potentials of the zeros to the right of the line, so \(\mathrm{QRH}(\theta)\) holds exactly when the defect is zero at one height, and at \(\theta=\frac12\), \(t_0=0\) this is the formula of Balazard, Saias and Yor.
  • The reciprocal of the completed Davenport and Heilbronn function is infinitely divisible with positive Levy density but is not an extended generalised gamma convolution (Volume II, Theorem 14.4.3, page 325): The Levy density of the reciprocal of the completed Davenport and Heilbronn function is positive for every \(u\ne0\) although the function has zeros off the line, so infinite divisibility of the reciprocal does not force the zeros onto the line; the proof rests on three finite zero computations certified in ball arithmetic.
  • Voronoi and Delaunay weighted Monte Carlo at independent uniform nodes for integrands flat at the boundary: root mean square error O(N^(-1/2-2/d)) in every dimension d (Volume II, Theorem 14.7.3, page 348): For \(g\) of class \(C^3\) on the cube \([-1,1]^d\) that vanishes near the boundary or is smooth with every derivative vanishing there, the Voronoi rule and the Delaunay rule at \(N\) independent uniform nodes have bias \(O(N^{-k})\) for every \(k\) (exponentially small in the first case) and variance \(O(N^{-1-4/d})\), so root mean square error \(O(N^{-1/2-2/d})\): \(N^{-5/2}\) on the line and \(N^{-3/2}\) in the plane.
