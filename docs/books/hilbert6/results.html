Chapter 7. Scale Mixtures, Thorin Measures and Shot Noise

Pólya Gamma integral identity for the logistic likelihood

Theorem 7.5, page 85 of Polson and Sokolov (2026), On Hilbert’s 6th Problem: Probability, Mechanics and Bayesian Computation, edition 1. First stated in Polson, Scott and Windle (2013), Bayesian inference for logistic models using Pólya Gamma latent variables, Journal of the American Statistical Association 108, 1339 to 1349.

Status: proved.

Statement. For \(b>0\) and \(c\in\mathbb{R}\), the Pólya Gamma law \(PG(b,c)\) is the law of \(\omega=\frac{1}{2\pi^2}\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{g_k}{(k-\frac12)^2+c^2/(4\pi^2)}\), where \(g_1,g_2,\dots\) are independent Gamma variables with shape \(b\) and unit rate. Let \(p(\cdot\mid b,0)\) be the density of \(PG(b,0)\). Then for all \(b>0\), \(a\in\mathbb{R}\) and \(\psi\in\mathbb{R}\), \(\frac{(e^{\psi})^a}{(1+e^{\psi})^b}=2^{-b}e^{(a-b/2)\psi}\int_0^\infty e^{-\omega\psi^2/2}\,p(\omega\mid b,0)\,\mathrm{d}\omega\). Moreover, the conditional law of \(\omega\) given \(\psi\), that is the law with density proportional to \(e^{-\omega\psi^2/2}p(\omega\mid b,0)\) in \(\omega\), is \(PG(b,\psi)\).

Horseshoe density bounds

Theorem 7.9, page 89 of Polson and Sokolov (2026), On Hilbert’s 6th Problem: Probability, Mechanics and Bayesian Computation, edition 1. First stated in Carvalho, Polson and Scott (2010), The horseshoe estimator for sparse signals, Biometrika 97, 465 to 480.

Status: proved.

Statement. Let \(\theta\mid\lambda\sim N(0,\lambda^2)\) with \(\lambda\) half Cauchy \(C^+(0,1)\) (the horseshoe prior with global scale \(\tau=1\)). The marginal density of \(\theta\) is \(p(\theta)=K\,e^{\theta^2/2}E_1(\theta^2/2)\), where \(K=1/\sqrt{2\pi^3}\) and \(E_1(x)=\int_x^\infty s^{-1}e^{-s}\,\mathrm{d}s\) is the exponential integral, and it satisfies, for all \(\theta\neq0\), \(\frac K2\log\big(1+\frac4{\theta^2}\big)<p(\theta)<K\log\big(1+\frac2{\theta^2}\big)\). In particular \(p\) has a logarithmic pole at zero and tails of order \(\theta^{-2}\).

Gradient stability for the initial condition of two dimensional Navier Stokes, with time zero observed

Proposition 12.8, page 153 of Polson and Sokolov (2026), On Hilbert’s 6th Problem: Probability, Mechanics and Bayesian Computation, edition 1. New in this book.

Status: proved.

Statement. Let \(\nu,T,L>0\). Let \(\mathbb{T}^2=(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})^2\), let \(\dot H^{s}_\diamond\) denote the Sobolev space of order \(s\) of divergence free, mean zero vector fields on \(\mathbb{T}^2\), and let \(f\in\dot H^{1}_\diamond\) be a forcing. Let \(\mathcal{X}=[0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^2\) with the uniform probability measure, and let \(e_1,e_2,\dots\) be the eigenfunctions of the Stokes operator on \(\mathbb{T}^2\), orthonormal in \(L^2\), with eigenvalues \(\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\cdots\). For \(D\ge1\) and \(\theta\in\mathbb{R}^D\) let \(\mathcal{G}(\theta)(t,x)=u_\theta(t,x)\) be the velocity of the solution of the two dimensional Navier Stokes equations with viscosity \(\nu\), forcing \(f\) and initial condition \(\sum_{k\le D}\theta_ke_k\). Here \(v^\top\nabla\mathcal{G}(\theta)=\sum_{k\le D}v_k\,\partial\mathcal{G}(\theta)/\partial\theta_k\) is the derivative of \(\mathcal{G}\) at \(\theta\) in the direction \(v\) and \(|v|\) is the Euclidean norm.

Bernstein von Mises for generators

Proposition 12.10, page 157 of Polson and Sokolov (2026), On Hilbert’s 6th Problem: Probability, Mechanics and Bayesian Computation, edition 1. New in this book.

Status: proved.

Statement. Let \(\Pi_n(\cdot\mid y)\) be posterior distributions of a parameter \(\theta\) given data \(y\), let \(L\) be a functional of the parameter, let \(\hat L_n\) be a statistic, and let \(H_n\) be a generator, a map that takes the data \(y\) and a uniform random variable \(U\) to a parameter value \(H_n(y,U)\). Write \(\tilde\Pi_n\) for the law of \(\sqrt n\,(L\theta-\hat L_n)\) when \(\theta\sim\Pi_n(\cdot\mid y)\), write \(\tilde G_n\) for the law of \(\sqrt n\,(L H_n(y,U)-\hat L_n)\) when \(U\) is uniform, and let \(d_{BL}\) be the bounded Lipschitz metric. Suppose that, in probability under the data generating law, \(d_{BL}\big(\tilde\Pi_n,N(0,\sigma^2)\big)\to0\) and \(d_{BL}\big(\tilde G_n,\tilde\Pi_n\big)\to0.\) Then \(d_{BL}\big(\tilde G_n,N(0,\sigma^2)\big)\to0\) in probability: the generated law satisfies the same Bernstein von Mises theorem as the posterior.

Manifold gain for the heat equation: prior on the initial condition against a space time Gaussian process prior

Proposition 14.5, page 193 of Polson and Sokolov (2026), On Hilbert’s 6th Problem: Probability, Mechanics and Bayesian Computation, edition 1. New in this book.

Status: proved.

Statement. Consider the heat equation \(\partial_tu=\nu\Delta u\) with viscosity \(\nu>0\) on the torus \(\mathbb{T}^d=[0,1]^d\), where \(\lambda_j\) are the eigenvalues of \(-\Delta\) (\(\lambda_j=4\pi^2|k_j|^2\) for the Fourier mode \(k_j\in\mathbb{Z}^d\setminus\{0\}\)), \(\lambda_1\) is the smallest, and \((e_j)\) is the corresponding orthonormal eigenbasis. There are \(N\) observations at design points uniform on \([0,T]\times\mathbb{T}^d\) with noise variance \(\varsigma^2\), the variance that the model normalizes to one and that is carried as a symbol only to show where it enters. In the constrained model the prior is placed on the initial condition, with independent Gaussian coefficients of variances \(\sigma_{0,j}^2=\rho^2(\lambda_j/\lambda_1)^{-\alpha}\), where \(\alpha>(d+1)/2\), so that the field is \(\sum_j\theta_je^{-\nu\lambda_jt}e_j\). In the unconstrained model, for comparison, the prior is a Gaussian process on the space time field \(u(t,x)\) with Fourier variances \(\sigma_{0,km}^2=\rho^2((\lambda_k+\omega_m^2)/\lambda_1)^{-\alpha}\), \(\omega_m=2\pi m/T\), \(m\in\mathbb{Z}\), where \(k\) runs over the spatial modes (those with \(\lambda_k\ge\lambda_1\)), so that the two priors have comparable regularity in \(x\). The Gram matrix of the design is replaced by its limit: \(\operatorname{diag}(\iota_j)\) with \(\iota_j=(1-e^{-2\nu\lambda_jT})/(2\nu\lambda_jT)\) in the constrained model, and the identity in the unconstrained one. Then: (i) the constrained posterior standard deviation of \(u(t,x)\) is \(\big(\sum_je^{-2\nu\lambda_jt}(N\iota_j/\varsigma^2+\sigma_{0,j}^{-2})^{-1}\big)^{1/2}\), decreasing in \(t\), and the effective number of parameters, \(\#\{j:N\iota_j\sigma_{0,j}^2\ge\varsigma^2\}\), is, for \(\varsigma=1\), of order \((N\rho^2/\nu T)^{d/(2\alpha+2)}\) as \(\Lambda=(N\rho^2\lambda_1^\alpha/(2\nu T))^{1/(\alpha+1)}\to\infty\) with \(\nu T\Lambda\geq1\), with constants that depend only on \(\alpha\), \(d\) and \(\rho\); (ii) the unconstrained posterior standard deviation is \(\big(\sum_{k,m}(N/\varsigma^2+\sigma_{0,km}^{-2})^{-1}\big)^{1/2}\), independent of \((t,x)\), and its effective number of parameters, \(\#\{(k,m):N\sigma_{0,km}^2\ge\varsigma^2\}\), is of order \(N^{(d+1)/(2\alpha)}\) as \(N\to\infty\); (iii) the ratio of the unconstrained to the constrained standard deviation at time \(t\) is at least \(e^{\nu\lambda_1t}\) times its value at \(t=0\), so it grows without bound as \(t\) increases, at the rate at which the manifold contracts.

Statement. Exact sufficiency, linear case

Proposition 15.2, page 207 of Polson and Sokolov (2026), On Hilbert’s 6th Problem: Probability, Mechanics and Bayesian Computation, edition 1. New in this book.

Status: proved.

Statement. Consider the linear model \(Y=\Phi\theta+\varepsilon\) with \(\theta\in\mathbb{R}^D\), design matrix \(\Phi_{ij}=s_j(t_i)e_j(x_i)\) (\(i\le N\), \(j\le D\), where \(S_te_j=s_j(t)e_j\) for a semigroup that is diagonal in the basis \((e_j)\)), noise \(\varepsilon\sim N(0,\mathrm{Id}_N)\) and Gaussian prior \(\theta\sim N(0,\Sigma_0)\). Let \(S_N=\Phi^\top Y\) be the adjoint statistic (the coefficient vector of \(w(0)\), where \(w\) solves the backward adjoint problem driven by the data) and \(V_N=(\Phi^\top\Phi+\Sigma_0^{-1})^{-1}\). Then \(S_N\) together with the design is sufficient for \(\theta\); the posterior given \(S_N\) is exactly the posterior given all the data, namely \(N(V_NS_N,V_N)\); and the conditional quantile map is exactly affine: for each coordinate \(j\) and \(\tau\in(0,1)\), \(Q_j(\tau\mid S_N)=(V_NS_N)_j+z_\tau(V_N)_{jj}^{1/2}\), where \(z_\tau\) is the \(\tau\) quantile of the standard normal law. The slope of this map in \(S_N\) is \(V_N\), not the identity. For the preconditioned summary \(\tilde S_N=V_NS_N\), which is the posterior mean, the map \(\tilde S_N\mapsto Q_j(\tau\mid S_N)\) has slope exactly one and, apart from the data independent scale term \(z_\tau(V_N)_{jj}^{1/2}\), intercept zero. Consequently generative Bayesian computation (GBC) with summary \(\tilde S_N\) and an identity plus scale generator is exact for every \(N\), up to the estimation of the \(D\times D\) scale from \(M\) simulations; in the linear model that scale is \(V_N\) itself and is known in closed form.

Equivalence of the one step efficient score summary with the efficient centering

Proposition 15.5, page 210 of Polson and Sokolov (2026), On Hilbert’s 6th Problem: Probability, Mechanics and Bayesian Computation, edition 1. New in this book.

Status: proved.

Statement. Let \(\Omega=[0,1]^d\) be the torus, \(T>0\), \(\mathcal X=[0,T]\times\Omega\), and let \(\mathscr{G}(\theta)=u_\theta\) be the solution map of an evolution equation with initial condition \(\theta\) and values in \(W\). The data are \(Z^{(N)}=(Y_i,X_i)_{i\le N}\) with \(Y_i=\mathscr{G}(\theta)(X_i)+\varepsilon_i\), \(X_i\) i.i.d. uniform on \(\mathcal X\) and \(\varepsilon_i\sim N(0,I_W)\) independent. Let \((e_j)\) be the orthonormal Laplace eigenbasis of mean zero functions, with eigenvalues \(\lambda_j\asymp j^{2/d}\); let the prior \(\Pi\) be the law of \((\sqrt N\delta_N)^{-1}\sum_j\lambda_j^{-\gamma/2}g_je_j\) with \(g_j\) i.i.d. \(N(0,1)\) and \(\delta_N=N^{-\gamma/(2\gamma+d)}\), and \(\Pi_N=\Pi(\cdot\mid Z^{(N)})\) the posterior; let \(\theta_0\) be the true initial condition, \(P^N_{\theta_0}\) the law of the data, \(\mathbb{I}_\vartheta\) the linearization of \(\mathscr{G}\) at \(\vartheta\), \(\mathcal{N}_\vartheta=N(0,T(\mathbb{I}_\vartheta^*\mathbb{I}_\vartheta)^{-1})\), \(J_N\asymp N\delta_N^2\) and \(p_N\) the projection onto \(\operatorname{span}\{e_j:j\le J_N\}\).

Suppose Conditions 1 and 2 of Nickl (2024) hold with integer \(\gamma>\max(3\zeta+3d/2+2,\eta_0)\) and \(k>\max(\zeta+5d/2,\eta_0)\) as in his Bernstein von Mises theorem (\(\eta_0\) the index of his invertibility condition), that is, the prior above with Sobolev reproducing kernel space \(H^\gamma\) and, for the forward map on balls of \(H^{\bar\gamma}\), uniform \(L^\infty\) bounds, \(L^2\) Lipschitz continuity, a linearization at \(\theta_0\) with quadratic remainder, \(L^\infty\) mapping properties from \(H^\zeta\) with \(\zeta>d/2\), the stability estimate \(\|\mathscr{G}(\theta)-\mathscr{G}(\theta_0)\|_{L^2(\mathcal X)}\ge c\|\theta-\theta_0\|_{H^{-1}}\) and an invertible information operator (\(\bar\gamma\) is the Sobolev index of the localization ball, \(\max(2,2\zeta)<\bar\gamma<\gamma-\max(1,d/2)\)). Let \(\kappa\) satisfy \(2d+\zeta<\kappa<k-d/2\).

The one step efficient score summary is defined by cross fitting. The sample is split into two halves with index sets \(\mathcal I_1,\mathcal I_2\) of sizes differing by at most one. For \(b=1,2\) a preliminary estimate \(\bar\theta^{[b]}_N\) is computed from half \(b\) alone, takes values in the parameter space and satisfies \(\|\bar\theta^{[b]}_N-\theta_0\|_{H^\xi}=O_P(\tilde\delta_N(\xi))\) for \(0\le\xi\le\bar\gamma\), where \(\tilde\delta_N(\xi)=\delta_N^{(\bar\gamma-\xi)/(\bar\gamma+1)}\). With \(\bar e_j^{\,\vartheta}=T(\mathbb{I}_\vartheta^*\mathbb{I}_\vartheta)^{-1}e_j\), put for \(j\le J_N\) \(S_{N,j}=\frac12\sum_{b=1,2}\big[\langle e_j,\bar\theta^{[b]}_N\rangle+\frac1{|\mathcal I_{3-b}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_{3-b}}\langle\mathbb{I}_{\bar\theta^{[b]}_N}p_N(\bar e_j^{\,\bar\theta^{[b]}_N})(X_i),\,Y_i-u_{\bar\theta^{[b]}_N}(X_i)\rangle_W\big]\) and \(\hat S^{J_N}_N=\sum_{j\le J_N}S_{N,j}e_j\). The efficient centering of Nickl’s proof is \(\hat\theta_N=\sum_{j\ge1}\hat\Psi_{N,j}e_j\) with \(\hat\Psi_{N,j}=\langle e_j,\theta_0\rangle+\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\langle\mathbb{I}_{\theta_0}p_N(\bar e_j^{\,\theta_0})(X_i),\varepsilon_i\rangle_W\).

Suppose further that \(\sqrt N\,\tilde\delta_N(0)\tilde\delta_N(\zeta)\to0\); that \(\gamma<(\bar\gamma+1)(\kappa-d/2)\) unless \(\mathbb{I}^*_{\theta_0}\mathbb{I}_{\theta_0}\) is diagonal in \((e_j)\) or the preliminary estimates converge at the rate \(\delta_N\) in \(H^{-1}\); and that the weights depend on the linearization point in a Lipschitz way: for every \(B>0\) there is \(L=L(B)\) such that for all \(\vartheta\) with \(\|\vartheta\|_{H^{\bar\gamma}}\le B\) and all \(j\le J_N\), \(\|\mathbb{I}_\vartheta p_N(\bar e_j^{\,\vartheta})-\mathbb{I}_{\theta_0}p_N(\bar e_j^{\,\theta_0})\|_{L^2(\mathcal X)}\le L\,\|\vartheta-\theta_0\|_{H^\zeta}\|e_j\|_{H^{\kappa+2}}\). Then \(\sqrt N\,\|\hat S^{J_N}_N-\hat\theta_N\|_{H^{-k-2}}\to0\) in \(P^N_{\theta_0}\) probability, and consequently \(\mathscr{W}_1\big(\operatorname{Law}(\sqrt N(\theta-\hat S^{J_N}_N)\mid Z^{(N)}),\mathcal{N}_{\theta_0}\big)\to0\) in \(P^N_{\theta_0}\) probability, where \(\mathscr{W}_1\) is the Wasserstein distance of order one on \(H^{-k-2}\).

Sobolev rates of the one step summary under the efficient centering hypotheses

Corollary 15.9, page 217 of Polson and Sokolov (2026), On Hilbert’s 6th Problem: Probability, Mechanics and Bayesian Computation, edition 1. New in this book.

The book’s decomposition of the error of the localized generator reads, under Conditions 2A to 2D of Nickl (2024) and the hypotheses of the book’s theorem on the residual law under a localized proposal (a linear Gaussian model whose prior covariance and Gram matrix are diagonalized by one orthonormal basis), and with the residuals centered so that their mean is removed, \(\mathcal{E}(c)^2\asymp\beta(c)^2+a_v/c^{v}+b\,c^4\). Here \(\mathcal{E}(c)\) is the Wasserstein error of the localized generator, in posterior standard deviations; \(v=0\) for the identity head (the head that adds to the summary a Gaussian whose covariance is fitted to the training residuals) and \(v=2\) for an affine head (an affine function of the summary fitted by regression, whose slope is estimated from a design of spread \(c\)); \(a_v\asymp D/M\); \(b\asymp c_3^2/N\), with \(c_3\) the third derivative constant of the log likelihood in the Hessian metric; and \(\beta(c)=\frac1D\sum_{j\le D}\big|\sqrt{s_j+c^2p_j^2}-1\big|\), where \(p_1,\dots,p_D\) are the eigenvalues of the prior fraction operator \(P=\Sigma_N\Sigma_0^{-1}\) (so \(p_j\) is the fraction of the posterior precision in direction \(j\) supplied by the prior) and \(s_j=1-p_j\). The first term is the exact population bias of localization, the second is the Monte Carlo variance of the head, and the third is the leading term of a Taylor expansion of the relinearization error, so the decomposition is an expansion and not a two sided bound: the symbol \(\asymp\) matches orders term by term, with constants that are not tracked.

In the linear model \(Y=\Phi\theta+\varepsilon\), \(\varepsilon\sim N(0,\varsigma^2\operatorname{Id})\), with prior \(N(0,\Sigma_0)\), \(F=\Phi^\top\Phi/\varsigma^2\) and \(\Sigma_N=(F+\Sigma_0^{-1})^{-1}\), the summary \(S(Y)=\bar\theta+\Sigma_N\big(\Phi^\top(Y-\Phi\bar\theta)/\varsigma^2-\Sigma_0^{-1}\bar\theta\big)\) equals \(\Sigma_N(F\theta+\Phi^\top\varepsilon/\varsigma^2)\) for every point \(\bar\theta\): it is the posterior mean and does not depend on the linearization point. The term \(b\,c^4\) is therefore purely a nonlinear effect, and it arises because the summary is linearized once, at \(\bar\theta_N\). Iterating the Gauss Newton step means repeating the step above from each new iterate in place of \(\bar\theta_N\), with the Jacobian recomputed at the iterate from the data alone, at a cost of \(D+1\) forward solves per training draw for each extra step; recomputing it at the training parameter \(\theta^{(m)}\) would not define a statistic, since that parameter is not available for the observed data. The claim to be confirmed is that the decomposition then reduces to \(\beta(c)^2+a_v/c^{v}\), which for the affine head decreases in \(c\) until the bias \(\beta(c)\) begins to grow, so that the optimal radius moves out to where the localization bias binds, and the achievable error is within \(O(\sqrt{D/M})\) of the smallest value of \(\beta(c)\), the floor it approaches as \(M\) grows. A numerical confirmation exists: in the numerical study of Polson and Sokolov (2026), with a two step Gauss Newton summary for the six mode (\(D=6\)) two dimensional Navier Stokes problem of the book’s experiments, the \(c^4\) term disappeared, and the error of the affine head (Wasserstein distance to a Metropolis reference chain, in posterior standard deviations) at \(c=6\) fell from \(0.37\) to \(0.16\) and became flat in \(c\).

Confirm this: for the summary obtained by iterating the Gauss Newton step, with the Jacobian recomputed from the data alone at each iterate, show that the \(c^4\) term disappears from the decomposition for the converged iterate, find how fast it decays with the number of steps, and determine whether, in a nonlinear problem, the resulting error falls below the error of the Laplace approximation, an error of order \(c_3\sqrt{J^3/N}\) in the classical bounds, with \(c_3\) a bound on the third derivatives of the log likelihood and \(J\) the dimension of the parameter. Here the Laplace approximation is the Gaussian approximation of the posterior at the maximum a posteriori estimate, with covariance the inverse of the negative Hessian of the log posterior density there.

Statement. Open question. The Bayesian calibration framework of Kennedy and O’Hagan (2001), as made operational by Higdon and coauthors, runs the simulator at a design, fits a Gaussian process emulator of \(\theta\mapsto\mathscr{G}(\theta)\), adds a discrepancy process \(\delta\) and runs Markov chain Monte Carlo on \((\theta,\delta)\). The natural formulation is hierarchical, with \(\delta\) a Gaussian process of amplitude \(\sigma_N\to0\); for GBC it is trivial to include \(\delta\) in the simulation of the training pairs, and for the theory the admissible rate of \(\sigma_N\) is open.

Combine the transfer theorem with a discrepancy process of amplitude \(\sigma_N\) and identify the rate at which \(\sigma_N\) must tend to zero for the deterministic limit (the Gaussian limit of that theorem for dynamics without model error) to persist, connecting to the Gibbs smoother above and to the particle methods for state space models with static parameters (Storvik (2002); Carvalho, Johannes, Lopes and Polson (2010)). In the linear model without a latent state these particle systems collapse (the sufficient statistic is a deterministic function of the data, all weights are equal and there is nothing to resample), whereas with \(G\ne0\) the statistic depends on the sampled path, the weights are no longer equal, and the degeneracy of particles can occur.
