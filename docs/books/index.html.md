# Books

Books by Vadim Sokolov and coauthors. Each book is free to read as a PDF, and its source and code are on GitHub.

## [Bayesian Causality](https://vsokolov.org/books/bayesian_causality_book/) <small>Models, Accounting and the Bayesian Alternative to Double Machine Learning</small>

[![Bayesian Causality: Models, Accounting and the Bayesian Alternative to Double Machine Learning](https://vsokolov.org/books/bayesian_causality_book/cover.jpg)](https://vsokolov.org/books/bayesian_causality_book/) Nick Polson and Vadim Sokolov. 2026. Edition 1.

A textbook that builds one Bayesian model of outcomes given treatment and covariates and reads every causal quantity from its posterior, and compares it with double machine learning in simulations and in the 401(k), LaLonde and NHEFS data.

[![PDF](https://vsokolov.org/books/icon-pdf.svg)](https://github.com/VadimSokolov/bayesian_causality_book/releases/download/v1.2.1/bayesian_causality_book.pdf "PDF") [![Source on GitHub](https://vsokolov.org/books/icon-github.svg)](https://github.com/VadimSokolov/bayesian_causality_book "Source on GitHub") [![DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23985916](https://zenodo.org/badge/DOI/10.5281/zenodo.23985916.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23985916)

## [On the Collatz Conjecture](https://vsokolov.org/books/collatz/) <small>Martingales, Momentum and Continued Fractions</small>

[![On the Collatz Conjecture: Martingales, Momentum and Continued Fractions](https://vsokolov.org/books/collatz/cover.jpg)](https://vsokolov.org/books/collatz/) Nick Polson and Vadim Sokolov. 2026. Edition 1.

Why the Collatz (3x+1) conjecture resists proof: the map as a random walk, an optimizer and a Diophantine problem; proofs that fixed horizon monotone Lyapunov certificates and finite residue sieves cannot settle it and the cycle inequality cannot exclude all cycles; a momentum method from log2 3.

## [On Langlands Conjectures](https://vsokolov.org/books/langlands/) <small>Probabilistic Methods</small>

[![On Langlands Conjectures: Probabilistic Methods](https://vsokolov.org/books/langlands/cover.jpg)](https://vsokolov.org/books/langlands/) Nick Polson and Vadim Sokolov. 2026. Edition 1.

What probability can and cannot contribute to the Langlands programme: Sato Tate laws, random walks on trees and isogeny graphs, Thorin measures and generalized gamma convolutions as reformulations of the Riemann hypothesis, and Bayesian identification of Sato Tate groups, with code and data.

## [On Hilbert's 6th Problem](https://vsokolov.org/books/hilbert6/) <small>Probability, Mechanics and Bayesian Computation</small>

[![On Hilbert's 6th Problem: Probability, Mechanics and Bayesian Computation](https://vsokolov.org/books/hilbert6/cover.jpg)](https://vsokolov.org/books/hilbert6/) Nick Polson and Vadim Sokolov. 2026. Edition 1.

Hilbert's sixth problem from Kolmogorov's axioms and the Boltzmann equation to the Navier Stokes equations, and the inverse passage: Bayesian estimation on the solution manifold of a differential equation, where the adjoint evolution of the data is a sufficient statistic for linear equations.

## [P = RP](https://vsokolov.org/books/p_equals_rp/) <small>A Monte Carlo Program for P versus NP</small>

[![P = RP: A Monte Carlo Program for P versus NP](https://vsokolov.org/books/p_equals_rp/cover.jpg)](https://vsokolov.org/books/p_equals_rp/) Nick Polson and Vadim Sokolov. 2026. Edition 1.

N queens counting as a rare event and a quantile integral, turned into a Monte Carlo program for P versus NP: NP = RP is shown equivalent to sampling queens completions, promise RP = promise P, which implies P = RP, is restated as a hitting set problem, with exact computations on the queens.

## [On Statistical Methods for Genetics](https://vsokolov.org/books/statistical_methods_genetics/) <small>Fisher, Ewens and the Bayesian Programme</small>

[![On Statistical Methods for Genetics: Fisher, Ewens and the Bayesian Programme](https://vsokolov.org/books/statistical_methods_genetics/cover.jpg)](https://vsokolov.org/books/statistical_methods_genetics/) Nicholas G. Polson. 2026. Edition 1.

Statistical theory of population genetics: an exact form of Fisher's Fundamental Theorem, shrinkage estimates of the rate of adaptation, the Ewens sampling formula and its rigidity, rational conjugacy, and sufficiency for learned coalescent inference.
