Chapter 3. The Fundamental Theorem of Natural Selection: From Deterministic to Stochastic, Fisher to Bayes

Moving projection lemma

Lemma 3.2, page 63 of Polson (2026), On Statistical Methods for Genetics: Fisher, Ewens and the Bayesian Programme, edition 1. New in this book.

Status: proved.

Statement. Let \(P_{uv}(t)\) be smooth genotype frequencies at a locus, with no assumption of Hardy Weinberg proportions, let \(m_{uv}\) be fixed Malthusian fitnesses and \(\bar m=\sum_{u,v}P_{uv}m_{uv}\). Let \(A_{uv}=\beta_u+\beta_v\) be the fitted values of the \(L^2(P(t))\) projection of \(m\) onto the span of the gene contents, with average effects \(\beta_u\), and let \(\delta_{uv}=m_{uv}-A_{uv}\) be the residuals. Then \(\frac{d\bar m}{dt}=\sum_{u,v}\dot P_{uv}A_{uv}+\sum_{u,v}\dot P_{uv}\delta_{uv}=\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{R}\), where \(\mathcal{A}=2\sum_u\dot p_u\beta_u\) is Fisher’s term, with \(p_u\) the frequency of allele \(u\), and, since \(\sum_{u,v}P_{uv}\delta_{uv}=0\), the remainder is \(\mathcal{R}=\sum_{u,v}\dot P_{uv}\delta_{uv}=\langle\delta,\dot P/P\rangle_t\).

Whole genome Fundamental Theorem decomposition

Theorem 3.5, page 66 of Polson (2026), On Statistical Methods for Genetics: Fisher, Ewens and the Bayesian Programme, edition 1. New in this book.

Status: proved.

Statement. Let \(P_g(t)\) be genotype frequencies over a finite set of genotypes \(g\), with no assumption of Hardy Weinberg proportions or of linkage equilibrium, let \(m_g\) be the Malthusian fitness of genotype \(g\), let \(\bar m=\sum_g P_g m_g\), let \(V\) be the span of the gene content functions (the additive functions of the genotype), let \(\beta_{ij}\) be the whole genome average effects, defined as the coefficients of the \(L^2(P_t)\) projection of \(m\) onto \(V\), let \(\dot p_{ij}\) be the rates of change of the allele frequencies, and let \(\delta=m-\Pi_t m\) be the residual of that projection. Then \(\frac{d\bar m}{dt}=\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{R}\) with \(\mathcal{A}=2\sum_{i,j}\dot p_{ij}\beta_{ij}\) and \(\mathcal{R}=\sum_g\dot P_g\,\delta_g=\langle\delta,\partial_t\log P\rangle_t\). Moreover \(\mathcal{R}=0\) whenever \(\partial_t\log P\in V\), and if \(\mathcal{R}=0\) for every assignment of Malthusian parameters \(m\) then necessarily \(\partial_t\log P\in V\).

Exponential family factorization of the remainder

Theorem 3.8, page 69 of Polson (2026), On Statistical Methods for Genetics: Fisher, Ewens and the Bayesian Programme, edition 1. New in this book.

Status: proved.

Statement. Let the genotype distribution move within the family \(\log P_g(t)=\sum_jc_j(t)X_j(g)+\sum_A\theta_A(t)u_A(g)-\psi(c(t),\theta(t))\), where \(\{X_j\}\) spans the space \(V\) of gene contents and \(\{u_A\}\) completes the basis, and let \(\delta\) be the residual of the projection of fitness onto \(V\) in \(L^2(P_t)\). Then \(\langle\delta,\partial\log P/\partial c_j\rangle_t=0\) for every main effect \(j\), and \(\mathcal{R}=\sum_A\langle\delta,u_A\rangle_t\,\dot\theta_A\). Here \(\mathcal{R}=\sum_g\dot P_g\,\delta_g\) is the remainder in \(d\bar m/dt=\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{R}\), with \(\bar m\) the mean Malthusian fitness.

Corollary 3.10, page 70 of Polson (2026), On Statistical Methods for Genetics: Fisher, Ewens and the Bayesian Programme, edition 1. New in this book.

Status: proved.

Statement. The residual in closed form at a biallelic locus

Proposition 3.11, page 71 of Polson (2026), On Statistical Methods for Genetics: Fisher, Ewens and the Bayesian Programme, edition 1. New in this book.

Status: proved.

Statement. At a biallelic locus let the genotypes \(A_1A_1,A_1A_2,A_2A_2\) have frequencies \(P_{11},P_{12},P_{22}\), all positive (all three genotype classes present), and Malthusian fitnesses \(m_{11},m_{12},m_{22}\). Let \(V\) be the span of the gene contents, let \(\delta\) be the residual of the \(L^2(P)\) projection of \(m\) onto \(V\), let \(D=m_{11}-2m_{12}+m_{22}\), let \(\alpha=\log\{4P_{11}P_{22}/P_{12}^2\}\) be Lindley’s Hardy Weinberg disequilibrium coefficient, and let \(\mathcal{I}(\alpha)=(1/P_{11}+4/P_{12}+1/P_{22})^{-1}\) be the Fisher information about \(\alpha\) per genotyped individual. Write \(\sigma^2_G=\sum_{u,v}P_{uv}(m_{uv}-\bar m)^2\) for the total genetic variance, \(\sigma^2_D=\sum_{u,v}P_{uv}\delta_{uv}^2\) for the dominance variance and \(\mathcal{A}\) for the additive variance. Let \(h=(1/P_{11},\,-2/P_{12},\,1/P_{22})\). Then \(h\) spans the orthogonal complement of \(V\) in \(L^2(P)\), and \(\delta=D\,\mathcal{I}(\alpha)\,h\), \(\sigma^2_D=D^2\mathcal{I}(\alpha)\) and \(\mathcal{A}=\sigma^2_G-D^2\mathcal{I}(\alpha)\). For every velocity of the genotype frequencies, the remainder \(\mathcal{R}=\langle\delta,\dot P/P\rangle_t\) equals \(D\,\mathcal{I}(\alpha)\dot\alpha\). Under Hardy Weinberg proportions \(\sigma_D^2=(pqD)^2\), the classical dominance variance \((2pqd)^2\) in the parametrization \(m=(\mu+a,\mu+d,\mu-a)\) with \(D=-2d\).

Two locus residual

Proposition 3.14, page 73 of Polson (2026), On Statistical Methods for Genetics: Fisher, Ewens and the Bayesian Programme, edition 1. New in this book.

Status: proved.

Statement. For two biallelic loci let the haplotypes \(AB,Ab,aB,ab\) have positive frequencies \(x_1,\dots,x_4\) and Malthusian fitnesses \(m_1,\dots,m_4\), let \(n_A,n_B\in\{0,1\}\) be the gene contents and \(V=\operatorname{span}\{1,n_A,n_B\}\), and let \(\delta\) be the residual of the \(L^2(x)\) projection of \(m\) onto \(V\). Define \(\lambda=\log\{x_1x_4/(x_2x_3)\}\), the log odds ratio of the haplotype table, \(E=m_1-m_2-m_3+m_4\), the interaction contrast of fitness, \(\mathcal{I}(\lambda)=(\sum_i1/x_i)^{-1}\), the Fisher information about \(\lambda\) per haplotype, and \(D_{AB}=x_1x_4-x_2x_3\). In particular, recombination at rate \(r\) in continuous time, \(\dot x=-rD_{AB}(1,-1,-1,1)\), moves no allele frequency and changes mean fitness at the rate \(\mathcal{R}=-rD_{AB}E\), exactly.

Exact remainder for one locus with k alleles

Theorem 3.16, page 76 of Polson (2026), On Statistical Methods for Genetics: Fisher, Ewens and the Bayesian Programme, edition 1. New in this book.

Status: proved.

Statement. Consider discrete nonoverlapping generations, one locus with \(k\) alleles, random mating and constant genotypic fitnesses \(w_{ij}\), so that genotype frequencies are \(p_ip_j\). Write \(\bar w=\sum_{i,j}p_ip_jw_{ij}\), \(w_{i\cdot}=\sum_jp_jw_{ij}\), \(\alpha_i=w_{i\cdot}-\bar w\), \(\Delta p_i=p_i\alpha_i/\bar w\), and \(\delta_{ij}=w_{ij}-\bar w-\alpha_i-\alpha_j\). Let \(\mathcal{A}\) denote the additive term, \(\mathcal{A}=2\sum_ip_i\alpha_i^2\). Then \(\Delta\bar w=\frac{\mathcal{A}}{\bar w}+\frac{1}{\bar w^{2}}\sum_{i,j}\delta_{ij}(p_i\alpha_i)(p_j\alpha_j)\). With two alleles and \(D=w_{11}-2w_{12}+w_{22}\) this is \(\Delta\bar w=\mathcal{A}/\bar w+D(\Delta p)^2\).
