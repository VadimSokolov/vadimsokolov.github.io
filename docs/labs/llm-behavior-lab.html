How a language model behaves

It guesses the next token. A picture becomes tokens the same way a sentence does. Training changes which guess you get, and a badly chosen reward changes it in the wrong direction.

Guess the next word

The sentence so far is a list of tokens. The model puts a weight on each word that could come next. “Stay with the likely word” keeps picking the tallest bar. “Allow surprises” sometimes picks a shorter one.

A picture takes the same next step

Switch the drawing. Under it, the picture has already been cut into tokens. The next token is the model’s name for what it sees. The machinery did not change. Only the tokens in front of it changed.

Three stages, one question

The question is “What is the capital of France?” Each stage was trained with a different job.

Pre-training

Read a huge amount of text and practice guessing the next token.

“… Paris, a city of about two million on the Seine.”

Post-training

Shown questions paired with the shape of an answer.

“Paris.”

A reward for sounding thorough

Paid when the answer is long and confident, whether or not it is careful.

“A rich question. European capitals have long histories, and while Paris is often mentioned one should also weigh Lyon.”

Pre-training teaches what tends to come next. Post-training teaches the shape of an answer. A reward teaches whatever you paid for, including the wrong thing. Checking the reward is an evaluation problem, which is next week.
