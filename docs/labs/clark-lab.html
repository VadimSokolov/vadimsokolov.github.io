Sally Clark

Two infants in one family died. An expert told the court that two natural deaths were so rare the mother must be guilty. The jury was given the answer to a different question than the one they had to decide.

Numbers from the Sally Clark case in Chapter 2. A first sudden infant death in a non-smoking family is taken as 1 in 8,500. A second, given the first, is taken as 1 in 100, because the causes can run in a family. Two murders by a mother are taken as 1 in 1,000,000. The conviction was later quashed.

The question the jury needed

Both explanations are rare. Among families who suffered two deaths of this kind, the useful comparison is how many of those families are the natural explanation and how many are murder. Rarity by itself is not a chance of innocence. The expert’s “1 in 73 million” was widely heard as the chance Sally Clark was innocent. It was not.

Under the unrelated-deaths story, 72 of these 73 families would be murder. Under the shared-cause story, using the book’s figures, 46 of 100 would be murder and 54 natural. That is a coin toss, not a conviction. Her conviction was quashed on appeal.
