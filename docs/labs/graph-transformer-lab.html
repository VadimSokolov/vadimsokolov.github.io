Graph Transformer Lab: the Medici and the Ambanis

In 1434 Cosimo de' Medici took control of Florence. His family was not the richest and did not hold the most council seats. Padgett and Ansell's answer is position: in the marriage network the Medici sat between families that had no other way to reach each other. This lab trains nothing in Florence. Four small transformers learned to predict betweenness on random graphs and are applied, unchanged and live in your browser, to the Florentine families, then to the Ambani family and Reliance six centuries later. They differ only in where the graph enters the attention step. Edit the networks and watch all four answer.

The weights of the paper's four models from each of its five training seeds (3,000 steps of 64 random graphs each) run here in JavaScript; the first seed loads with the page and the other four when you ask for them. Florentine data: Padgett, as distributed in the R package ergm (wealth, priorates and the 15 business ties checked against ergm's florentine file); marriages from networkx. The Ambani graphs are the companion deck's hand coding from press reports and company announcements through 2026.

Y = F(X, A)

A transformer is a composite map \(Y = F(X)\). On a graph the input comes with an adjacency matrix \(A\) saying who is tied to whom. The one new design decision is where in the composite map \(A\) enters.

Four answers, one code path

Set: not at all, each node sees only its degree. Local: as a mask, attend to partners only (GCN, GAT). Laplacian: eigenvectors added to the input. Distance bias: a learned number added to each attention score by graph distance (Graphormer).

Learn the rule elsewhere

Fifteen families cannot train a transformer. The models learned betweenness on 192,000 random graphs of 10 to 20 nodes. Florence and Mumbai are never used for fitting: zero-shot transfer, and we know the exact answer to check it.

Four ways to let the graph in

Each model is 4 attention layers of width 64 with 4 heads, about 139 thousand parameters, fed each node's degree and nothing else about the families. The attention score between nodes \(i\) and \(j\) is\[ a_{ij} = \operatorname{softmax}_j\Bigl( \tfrac{q_i \cdot k_j}{\sqrt{d}} + b_{\mathrm{dist}(i,j)} \Bigr), \]with \(b \equiv 0\) for Set and Laplacian, \(b = -\infty\) beyond distance one for Local, and \(b\) learned for Distance bias. Laplacian adds the first 8 nontrivial eigenvectors of the normalized Laplacian to the input. The bars are the exact betweenness of the graph above, the dots the four predictions, computed now in your browser.

Pick a single trained model or the mean of all five seeds; with all five, a line through each dot spans the seeds, and the hub column counts the seeds that name the exact most-between node. The last row ranks nodes by degree alone, the baseline the Set model reproduces. Training on random graphs

All four models saw the same stream of graphs: uniform random graphs, preferential attachment, and random trees with a few chords, 10 to 20 nodes each. The loss is squared error on every node's betweenness. Error is measured on 500 held-out random graphs, in units of betweenness. The paper trains each model from five seeds; the table reports the mean and the spread.

Where transfer breaks

Florence and Mumbai are single graphs. To see how each architecture fails, the paper scores the same trained models on random graphs that differ from the training graphs in one respect each: larger (25 to 40 nodes against 10 to 20), denser (1.8 to 2.6 ties per node, beyond the training maximum of 1.8), and role graphs that imitate a control network, people tied to businesses at random with a small, densely tied kin group. Draw one and read the scatter, or score forty and compare with the paper's 300 graphs and five seeds. The models never saw a graph with more than 20 nodes; in the browser they run on any size.

What carries over

  1. Position, not attributes

    The Medici were neither the richest nor the most seated family. They were the only route between groups. Betweenness sees that; a model of each family's own row cannot.

  2. Where A enters is the design

    Same width, depth, optimiser and training graphs; only the way the graph enters changes. That is an ablation, and it is the honest way to compare architectures.

  3. A mask is a dogmatic prior

    Attention is a learned kernel. The distance bias is a prior on that kernel that lets the data say how much distance matters. The mask sets every \(b\) beyond distance one to \(-\infty\) before seeing any data.

  4. No architecture dominates

    The distance bias model is best on held-out graphs and role graphs, the Local model is the only one that names the right hub of the Reliance control graph in every seed, and the Local model is the worst on larger graphs, where four layers of message passing cannot see across. Which one transfers best depends on how the new graph differs from the training graphs, which is rarely known in advance. Betweenness has an exact algorithm that runs in microseconds: check before you trust.

De Roover's bank is a second star

Raymond de Roover rebuilt the Medici bank from its own account books. Each branch (Rome, Venice, Geneva, Bruges, London and others) was a separate partnership tied to Florence and not to the other branches, with the Medici senior partners in every one. A loss in one could not legally sink the rest. A hub collects information and control; it is also a single point of failure, and the bank was gone by 1494. Reliance's one parent with a family member at the head of each business has the same shape.

Broker or closed group

Gould and Fernandez (1989) split brokerage into five roles by which groups the three parties belong to: betweenness asks how often a node is in the middle, they ask in the middle of whom. The Strozzi, Peruzzi, Castellani and Bischeri marry among themselves: many ties, many closed triangles, no single broker. Gould's Corsican feuds are the closed-group kind of power; Blok and Gambetta's mafia combines a closed group inside with a broker outside.

Cautions

The Ambani graphs are an ego network grown out from the family, so the family is central by construction: only comparisons inside it mean much. Ties are unweighted, and shareholdings and family trusts are not in the graph; on weighted ties the chairman would not look redundant. Coding is judgement: another coder gets another graph. And the models predict betweenness, nothing else. Betweenness is not power.

Try this in class

  1. Remove the Medici. Which family becomes the most between, and how far does the graph fall apart? Then restore them and remove the Guadagni instead.
  2. Add a single marriage between the Strozzi and the Albizzi. How much betweenness do the Medici lose? Find the one new tie that hurts the Medici most.
  3. In the features panel, which family would a wealth-only model put first? Why does the Set model, which sees only degrees, still promote the Strozzi?
  4. Switch to the business layer. Which models still rank well, and why might four families with no ties at all make it harder?
  5. Look at the learned distance bias for each layer. Which distances does the model look at hardest, and does that change with depth? How would you have to set \(b\) to reproduce the Local model?
  6. In the houses graph, remove the founder house. What happens to the graph and to the betweenness of Mukesh's house? Relate it to 2002 and 2005.
  7. In the control graph, remove Mukesh. Does the top of the ranking change? Which single further removal disconnects the graph? Which model's error grows most on these denser graphs, and why?
  8. Choose "All five" seeds. On the marriage graph, which model's predictions vary most across seeds? On the houses graph, why does ranking by degree have the highest rank correlation and still name the wrong hub?
  9. In "Where transfer breaks", score forty larger graphs, then forty denser ones. Which model loses most with size, and why does density do no harm?
  10. Relational attention, on paper: add a second bias indexed by the kind of tie (none, marriage, business, both). Write the new score \(a_{ij}\) and count the extra parameters.
