Poisson Match Predictor

Each team's goals are a Poisson count. Its rate λ is the league average times its attack strength times the opponent's defence weakness. Multiply the two Poisson distributions to get every scoreline, add them up to get win, draw and loss, and compare with the bookmaker.

From ratings to a score matrix to a bet

The model multiplies three numbers for each team. ManU: 1.47 × 1.46 × 1.37 = 2.94 (often rounded to 2.95). Hull: 1.47 × 0.85 × 0.52 = 0.65. A run of 100 simulated matches gave ManU 88 wins and put 0–3 on top. The exact calculation gives 83.4% and puts 0–2 just ahead of 0–3. One hundred matches is a small sample: the standard error of a proportion near 0.85 is about 3.6 points.

  1. Goals are Poisson counts

    Goals are rare events in a fixed period, so the count is close to Poisson with rate λ. Mean and variance are both λ, which the stats table lets you check from the simulation.

  2. Ratings set each λ

    λ for a team = league average × its attack strength × the opponent's defence weakness. A strong attack against a leaky defence multiplies up.

  3. Independence gives the matrix

    With independent goal counts, P(Hull = i, ManU = j) = P(Hull = i) · P(ManU = j). Cells below the diagonal are Hull wins, the diagonal is draws, and cells above are ManU wins.

  4. Compare with the price

    A bookmaker's decimal price d implies probability 1/d. The implied probabilities add to more than 1; the extra is the overround, or vig. Bet only where your model's p is above the implied probability, so p·d − 1 > 0.

Takeaway for students. You do not need to predict the exact score. The actual result, 0–1, had only an 8% chance in this model. What matters is whether a price is wrong. If the book offers ManU at 1.25, it implies 80%; the model says 83%, so the bet has a small positive edge. A Polymarket price has no house margin, so it is closer to a true probability than any single bookmaker's odds.

Try this in class

  1. Press "Simulate 100 matches" several times. Watch ManU's win count move between about 76 and 91, and the top score switch between 0–2 and 0–3.
  2. Press "Simulate 1,000 matches" and check that the mean and variance of ManU goals are both close to 2.94, as a Poisson count should be.
  3. Raise Hull's attack strength from 0.85 to 1.50. Ask the class how much the draw probability moves before you show it.
  4. Change the ManU price from 1.25 to 1.15. The implied probability rises to 87% and the edge turns negative. The rule: bet only when the price implies less than the model.
  5. Load "Two average teams". 1–1 becomes the most likely single score, yet a draw is still the least likely of the three outcomes.
